Method Matters

Squeegee vs. Water Fed Pole: Which One Do You Need

A squeegee is still the standard tool for most ground floor homes. It's fast, it works well on smaller jobs, and a skilled cleaner can leave glass completely streak free with nothing but a bucket and a blade.

Water fed pole systems work differently. Pure water runs through a long pole with a brush on the end, scrubbing the glass and rinsing it at the same time. Since the water has no minerals in it, the glass dries without spots and doesn't need a squeegee pass at all. This method is common for two storey homes or buildings where a ladder isn't safe or practical.

For anything higher than what a pole can reach, some companies use rope access, though this is rare for standard homes and mostly used on tall commercial buildings.

Timing

How Often Should Windows Be Cleaned in a City Like This

There's no single answer, since it depends on where the property sits. Homes close to the coast collect salt spray fast, and that film builds up within a few weeks if the wind has been strong. A clean every two to three months usually keeps coastal glass looking clear.

Inland homes away from the coast can often stretch to twice a year without the glass looking neglected, unless there are trees nearby dropping sap or pollen. Commercial buildings and shopfronts usually need more frequent attention, sometimes monthly, simply because dirty glass looks bad to customers walking past. Strata buildings often fall somewhere in between, with common area glass cleaned quarterly and unit windows cleaned less often unless requested.

Pricing

Window Cleaning Cost: What Actually Changes the Price

This is the part most people actually want to know, and it's rarely explained clearly. The number of windows matters most. A small unit with six windows costs far less than a two storey home with twenty or more.

Access changes price too. Ground floor windows are quick to reach and quick to clean. Second storey windows using a water fed pole take a bit longer to set up, which usually adds a small amount to the quote. Interior cleaning, if you want both sides done, roughly doubles the time compared to exterior only, since it's essentially two separate jobs happening in one visit.

Frequency also plays a role. Booking a regular clean every few months is often cheaper per visit than calling for a one off clean, since the provider isn't dealing with months of built up grime each time. As a rough guide, smaller homes might pay somewhere in the low to mid hundreds for a full exterior and interior clean, while larger homes or commercial sites cost more depending on window count and access. A proper quote after seeing the property is still the only accurate way to know the real number.

Access and Safety

What Changes With Height, and What to Check Before You Book

Anyone working above ground level is taking on some risk, and a proper provider should be able to show they've planned for it.

Ground Floor

The simplest job in the business. A cleaner reaches every pane standing on the ground, keeping time and cost low.

Multi Storey

Usually up to two or three levels, handled with a water fed pole instead of a ladder resting on the ground.

High Rise

Needs rope access or specialised lifting equipment, along with safety certifications beyond residential work.

Public Liability Insurance

Ask if the cleaner holds it, and don't just take a verbal answer at face value.

Working at Heights Training

Ask if staff hold this training, especially for anything above ground floor.

Damage Cover

Ask what happens if something gets damaged during the clean, before the job starts, not after.

By Property Type

Residential, Commercial, and Strata: What Changes

A home usually needs a straightforward clean every few months, focused on the main living area glass and any coastal salt buildup. Commercial buildings need a different rhythm entirely. Shopfronts often get cleaned weekly or fortnightly since dirty glass affects how a business looks to people walking past.

Strata buildings sit in the middle. Common area glass, like lobby windows and stairwell glass, usually gets scheduled cleaning as part of the building's maintenance plan, while individual unit windows are often left to each owner to arrange separately. This split catches a lot of new strata owners off guard, since they assume all glass is covered under the building's cleaning contract when it usually isn't. One-Off vs. Scheduled

One-Off Clean vs. Scheduled Cleaning: What Most Properties Actually Need

A one off clean makes sense before a big event, after renovation dust has settled, or simply when windows haven't been touched in a long time and need a proper reset. It costs more per visit since there's extra buildup to deal with.

Scheduled cleaning, usually every one to three months depending on location, keeps glass from ever reaching that heavily soiled point. It also tends to cost less per visit over time, since the job is faster when the windows were already clean a few months earlier. Most homeowners who try a one off clean first end up switching to a schedule once they see the difference in cost and effort over a year.
